Twelve citizens. 0.1 km² settled. 2,531.91 km² in reserve.

![The starting position in avenCITY world 85245 — a hex grid laid over a biome sandbox, almost all of it untouched.](/Day02/Overview%201.png)

That is the entire city on day one, and it is deliberate. maiaCITY is being built in a game before it is built in soil, because a game is the only place where you can run a settlement forward, watch it fail, and start again on Tuesday.

The world is a hex grid. Each hex is 750 m across and holds 36.5 hectares of land. You tap one, and it becomes yours to settle.

Zoning is set before a single dome goes down: 30% reserve, 25% works, 45% living. The reserve is not what is left over. It is what the city promises not to touch.

## A blueprint you cannot run is just a drawing

Most city plans are made at the scale of the finished city. A million people, a masterplan, a render with trees in it — and no way to test whether the thing feeds itself, because nobody builds a million-person city twice.

So the unit is wrong from the start. If the smallest thing you can design is a district, the smallest mistake you can make is a district.

We wanted the opposite: the smallest piece that is genuinely complete. Something that houses people, grows their food, makes something useful, and can be copied onto the next hex by people who were not in the room when we drew it.

One hex. One settlement. A *Siedlung* in the old sense — small, bounded, self-carrying.

## It starts with almost nothing

![Level 1 — six domes at 5 m across, twelve people, a fire ring and four shipping containers. 24 citizens, density 33/km².](/Day02/Level%201.png)

Level 1 is a camp. Six domes, five metres across, two people each. Containers for everything that isn't sleeping. A fire in the middle, because the middle matters more than the buildings.

The land is already doing the work. Of the hex's 36.5 hectares, 10 are settled — 27% — and the remaining 26.5 feed 265 people. Add 1.7 hectares of indoor beds inside the domes, which cover another 66 people and about 20% of a full diet, and the hex feeds 331.

Twelve people living on land that feeds 331. The surplus reads **+2,658%**.

![Level 2 — the domes grow to 8 m and hold four people each. 24 housed, 36 citizens, +1,279% surplus.](/Day02/Level%202.png)

Level 2 doubles the people without touching the footprint. Same hex, same 10 hectares, same food forest around it. The domes simply get better at holding people: 8 metres across, four to a dome.

The surplus halves twice over. Nobody notices yet.

## The land sets the ceiling, not the architect

![Level 3 — 40 m domes, twelve people each, 72 housed. A covered commons appears in the middle. Density 115/km².](/Day02/Level%203.png)

At Level 3 the settlement stops looking like a camp. Forty-metre domes, twelve people in each, a roofed commons at the centre, workshops along the edge. Seventy-two people housed on the same ground the twelve started on.

The surplus is down to +360%, and now the number means something. Every level up is a trade: more people inside the same ring, against the same 331 the land can feed.

![Level 4 — 70 m domes, 24 people each, 216 housed. The build yard is still running; density 312/km².](/Day02/Level%204.png)

Level 4 puts 216 people on the hex. Surplus: +53%. The food forest that looked infinite at Level 1 is now within sight of its limit, and it never moved. Only we did.

![Level 5 — one 136 m dome for 60 people surrounded by the rest. 276 housed, 288 citizens, +20% surplus.](/Day02/Level%205.png)

Level 5 is the cap. A 136-metre dome at the centre, the smaller ones gathered around it, 276 people housed and 288 living here. The surplus reads **+20%**, and that last 20% is the whole argument of this build.

A settlement is not limited by how tall you can build. It is limited by what its own land will feed — and the honest version of that number is visible before you place the first dome.

## One hex is a home. Two is a city.

![Hex −14,−8 — a neighbouring grove running solar works at 130 m across, next to a settlement of 360. Crop land appears for the first time.](/Day02/Overview%202.png)

A settlement that only feeds itself is a homestead. What makes it urban is the second hex.

Next door, the same primitive is running a different program: a works dome 130 metres across making solar panels, its own open land feeding 352, crop land showing up in the world header for the first time. Two hexes, two specialities, one grid — and the reserve still standing at 2,530.81 km².

That is the move. Not a masterplan that gets subdivided downward, but one unit that gets copied outward, each copy carrying its own food, its own roof and its own ceiling.

> The city is not the plan above the settlements. The city is what happens when enough settlements stand on their own and start trading.

Geodesic domes because they are cheap in material and honest about volume. A food forest because a permaculture homestead is the only kitchen that scales without a supply chain. A hex because it tiles, and because the boundary of your responsibility should be visible from inside it.

We are playing this first, at 60 frames per second, where a bad ceiling costs an afternoon instead of a decade.

Then we build it in soil.
