The clock in the corner of my screen says June 2028.

Outside my window it is still September 2026.

Neither clock is broken, and the gap between them is not a bug. It is the most useful thing we have built so far, and it took one failed evening to find out why.

## You cannot wait ten years to find out your money works

On [Day 09](/blog/day-09-hearts-and-minds/) I told you about hearts: money that every human creates for themselves, born from existing instead of from debt.

Here is the uncomfortable part about any money system. It is a bet on time.

A basic income only shows what it does after people have lived on it for months. A 7% yearly demurrage only shows what it does after a year. At Circles it took us two and a half years to learn what did not work, and by then the lesson had cost the project.

Maia City cannot learn that way. A city for a million co-founders has to be right before the first thousand move in.

So the question was never how to design hearts. It was how to watch a year of them before next year arrives, the way a time-lapse camera shows you a whole summer of a plant growing in thirty seconds.

## The first hearts came once an hour, and nothing happened

One evening I started building avenCITY Sandbox 2. I built it with AI coding agents at my side, and before the evening was over the first hearts existed.

Every citizen got their own currency, written as their name and a heart. Mine is Samuel♥. It paid one heart per hour, on top of a starting stake of 10,000.

It worked perfectly. It was also unbearable.

You opened the game, looked at your balance, and closed it again. Nothing moved. An economy that runs at the speed of the real world is an economy you cannot watch, and one you cannot watch you cannot fix.

## The next morning, I sped up time

Early the next morning I changed one number.

One real day became one game month. Twelve real days became a game year. A game day now lasts 48 real minutes, and the clock in the corner counts up from the city's founding thirty times faster than the one on your wall.

Hearts moved onto that clock. One heart per game hour, 24 per game day, which in real time is one heart every two minutes.

In the same change the starting stake dropped from 10,000 to 500. A fortune that size made every heart you earned afterwards meaningless. Five hundred is enough to begin with and small enough that the next heart still counts.

The ratio of thirty lives in exactly one place in the code. The clock reads it, the income reads it, and so do the "two hours ago" labels. When the world changes speed, everything in it changes speed together.

## Your first mint carries 500 hearts

When you join Sandbox 2, your wallet says 0.00.

The button at the bottom of the screen already says something else.

![A new citizen: 0.00 in the wallet, and +500 waiting on the Mint button.](/day-10-a-heart-every-two-minutes/first-login.jpg)

That +500 is the founding stake, the hearts every citizen enters the city with. It rides along on your first mint. Press it once and they are yours. The number on the button starts again from zero and ticks upward while you watch, a heart every two minutes.

![The first mint: 500 hearts, owed to nobody.](/day-10-a-heart-every-two-minutes/first-mint.jpg)

No bank lent them to you. No employer paid them. Nobody sent them. The ledger records them as coming from you, because they did.

Imagine a morning in your dome cell. The light comes through the glass of the centre dome, the food forest outside is loud with birds, and while you slept your hearts kept arriving, the same number as everyone else's. You mint them over breakfast, before you have decided what the day is for.

## Hearts you never collect stop coming

There is a limit, and it is on purpose.

Hearts pile up for 14 game days, about eleven real hours. After that the button stops counting. At most 336 hearts wait for you. Circles had the same rule for the same reason: money is for the present, not a savings plan you forgot about.

To see it, I stayed away for six real hours. That is 180 game hours.

![Six real hours away: 180 hearts waiting on the button.](/day-10-a-heart-every-two-minutes/mint-waiting.jpg)

Exactly 180 hearts, plus the few that came in while I was taking the picture.

## The hearts you keep slowly melt

Demurrage is the part people flinch at, so here is how it actually works.

Every game year, held hearts lose 7% of what they are worth. In Sandbox 2 a game year is twelve real days, so you can watch hoarding lose in under two weeks.

Think of it as bread, not gold. The amount written in your notes never changes. What changes is the value they show today, a little lower every day. New hearts rise in step, so 24 a day always buys what 24 a day bought before. A heart you earn today and a heart you earn next year buy the same. A heart you sit on for a year buys 7% less.

The effect is simple. Hearts want to move: into a neighbour's hands, into a coop, into something being built. Keeping them in a drawer is the only way to lose.

## Every heart is a sealed note

Under the button there is a ledger, and it is stricter than it looks.

Every mint creates a note, like a letter in a sealed envelope: an amount, an owner and a random number, sealed together into a single fingerprint. Spending a note publishes a second fingerprint that marks it as used without saying which note it was. Try to spend the same heart twice and the second fingerprint is already there. The spend is refused.

The shape comes from Zeto, the privacy token design that the Paladin project runs on. We chose it so the sandbox's notes can move onto a real privacy chain later without changing a single one. Today our server still checks the proofs itself. The notes are already the real shape.

## You do not claim your hearts. You create them.

For a while on the second morning the button was called Claim. It is Mint again, alone at the bottom centre of the screen, because you do not claim money somebody owes you. You create it.

Anyone can look at the whole planet without joining. Minting is the one thing that needs you to sign up, because every heart belongs to someone.

Imagine the day this leaves the game. Every child born in Maia City starts minting the moment they are named, the same hearts as their grandmother and the same as the founder of the oldest coop. Nobody is born owing anything.

That is where it starts: one heart every two minutes, a name on every one, and a clock fast enough to show us within twelve days whether it holds.

Your first 500 are already waiting on that button. Press it, and two minutes later watch the next heart arrive.

The hearts need somewhere to go next. In Sandbox 2 that means a place on the planet, a card of real land, and a coop standing on it.
