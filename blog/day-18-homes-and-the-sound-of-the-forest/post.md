The forest had no sound.

You could walk the whole dome cell, from the café squares to the far edge, past the hens and the stream and the goats, and hear nothing at all. It looked alive. It was silent.

And the rooms, up in the galleries where people are meant to live, were a bed and a table on a floor. You could walk straight through their walls.

## A room is not a bed and a table

On [Day 17](/blog/day-17-one-world-thirteen-domes/) every dome stood in one world. The rooms in them were still sketches.

A home is the corner where you read in the morning. It is the bath you step into after a day in the forest, the door you open onto the terrace, the plant on the shelf that needs water. So every room got the things a person actually lives with.

![A room in the gallery of a dome: rounded furniture, a timber floor, plants, and the clear glass beyond.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/room.jpg)

A wide bed with soft rounded edges. A curved bench along the wall with cushions on it, a round table and stools. A niche with shelves, a rug. A desk by the window with a deep armchair. And plants: in pots on the floor, on the shelves, climbing by the door.

None of it has a sharp corner. The furniture is plastered by hand, the way Waldorf buildings are, every edge rounded and every surface soft to the eye. You feel the difference before you can name it.

![The bed, the plants and the curved bench, the forest outside the glass.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/bed.jpg)

Every room has its own bath: a bowl of smooth stone, low and wide, set into the floor with pebbles round it. Behind a curved wall there is a shower, closed and warm.

![A stone bath in a room of the gallery, pebbles round it, an armchair beside it.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/bath.jpg)

![A desk and an armchair by the window.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/desk.jpg)

The walls between the rooms are solid now. You cannot walk through them, only through the door.

Imagine waking up in one of these. The light comes in grey-green through the leaves outside. You hear the stream before you open your eyes.

## The glass in front of your room is clear

On Day 17 the whole dome became solar glass, every triangle laid with dark blue cells. That is right for the roof. It is wrong for the view from your bed.

So the glass in front of the rooms is clear, and the solar cells stay above and around it. From your room you look straight out at the forest, the terrace and the sky. And in the glass there is a door.

![Looking back at a room's glass door from the terrace.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/door.jpg)

## Every terrace is wide enough to live on

The terraces used to be a walkway round the dome. A place to pass along, not to stay.

They are two and a half metres wider now, wide enough for a whole life outside. And along them, never the same set twice in a row, are four kinds of places to be:

- a round table for six under a pergola of vines, with round stools all round it
- two cushioned daybeds facing out over the forest, a low table between them
- a curved plaster bench round a low table, felt cushions, a big planter at each end
- two hanging egg chairs from a curved timber frame, a sheepskin in each

![The terrace in front of the rooms: daybeds, plants, the stone arcade.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/terrace.jpg)

![Daybeds on the terrace, looking out through the arcade.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/daybeds.jpg)

![The upper terrace of a large dome, high above the forest.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/terrace-upper.jpg)

Imagine an evening out here. Somebody from three doors down has brought a bowl of figs from the forest. The children are asleep inside, the glass door open a hand's width so you can hear them.

## Then the forest needed its voice

Rooms like that made the silence obvious. A home looks out at a forest. It should hear one.

I had the recordings: a forest in the morning, a stream over stones, hens, geese, a goat, frogs, a hive of bees. Playing them was easy. Making them sound like a place was not.

A sound in a real place comes from somewhere. The stream is quiet from the café square and loud on the bridge. The geese are a far call across the water until you walk round the pond and they are right there. So every sound in the cell now knows where it comes from, and it rises as you walk towards it and fades as you walk away. The forest is everywhere, softly. The rest belongs to a spot.

The recordings fought back. Each was made on a different day, with a different microphone, at a different distance. The goat was so quiet that at the same volume as the others you could not hear it at all: its recording sat more than twenty decibels below all the others. So each one was measured and brought up or down until they all stand at the same level. Only then could the mix be set by ear: the water a little louder, the forest a little softer, the geese a little nearer.

Step through a door and all of it changes. Under the glass, the outside is muffled, the way a room muffles a street, and a soft sound of leaves and birds fills the dome itself.

The solar factory dome sounds like a factory. Its halls hum, each machine gets louder as you pass it, and the lift makes its own sound as it carries you from floor to floor.

## So the animals came to where the sounds were

A sound needs someone making it.

There are more goats now, grazing in small flocks between the domes and along the paths.

![Goats beside a path between the domes.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/goats.jpg)

The geese keep to the water.

![Geese on the stream between the domes.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/geese.jpg)

Frogs sit on the banks of every pond and stream, and you hear them long before you see them. Fish swim in the ponds and the streams, and in the tanks of the aquaponics beds on the master dome's ground floor, where a small stream of its own runs through. And in the forest between the domes stand groups of beehives, the bees circling round them.

![Beehives in the food forest.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/hives.jpg)

## The ground was a green sheet

Walk through a real forest and look down. There is almost no plain ground in it.

So the forest floor got what a forest floor has: moss, the white threads of mycelium, patches of bare dark earth, ant hills, rotting logs going soft, stones and small rocks, low bushes. The grass got blades.

![The forest floor between the domes: grass, moss, stones and low plants under the trees.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/forest-floor.jpg)

![A rotting log on the forest floor.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/log.jpg)

The ponds were round blue discs. Now they have real shapes, shallow edges where the water is pale over sand and deep middles where it turns dark, reeds and lily pads along the shore. And no tree stands in the water anymore.

![A pond from above: its shallows and deep water, a bridge, reeds along the shore, a playground beside it.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/pond.jpg)

Between the domes stand three wooden playgrounds: a sandpit ringed with half-buried logs, a tower with a tent roof and a slide down into the sand, a swing on an A-frame of logs, stumps to jump between. The hives are never near them.

![A wooden playground between the domes, the master dome behind it.](/day-18-homes-and-the-sound-of-the-forest/playground.jpg)

## You can walk into any dome, any time

All of this made the cell heavier. And a heavier cell broke the promise from Day 17: that you walk up to a dome and step straight in.

With the new rooms, a large dome took longer to build than the walk to its door. You stood at the doorway and waited.

So the domes are no longer built only when you come near. From the moment you arrive, the game builds them in the background, the nearest first, a sliver of work in every frame so you never feel it, and it keeps the six you were at most recently. The dome you are walking towards is almost always ready long before you reach it. And when it is not, the door still opens: you walk in while the last of it arrives around you.

The other half was speed. With everything added, standing at the master dome had fallen to 15 frames a second, and every look around lagged behind the mouse. Three changes brought it back to about 40:

- **Only the dome you are at is drawn in full.** The others, built and waiting, show their simple selves until you come near.
- **Shadows are drawn when something moves them,** not sixty times a second.
- **The screen sharpens and softens with the load,** a little lower resolution when frames get slow, full again when there is room.

The hitch that came every time a new dome finished, half a second of the world standing still, is gone too. The game used to measure every piece of a new dome in one go. Now it measures a few pieces in each frame.

## Now it is a place

Imagine it tonight. You come back from the café square by the master dome with the path lights on, and the stream is loud at the bridge. The geese settle somewhere on the water. You climb the stairs to the gallery, open the glass door, and the forest goes quiet behind you. The bath is already full.

That is a home. Enough of them round one forest for 216 people is a dome cell.
