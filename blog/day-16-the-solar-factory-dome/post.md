Every triangle of glass in a maiaCITY dome starts as a handful of sand.

Not as a pallet from the other side of the world. Not as a container waiting in a port for a ship that is late.

As sand, and quartz, and a coil of copper, carried in through one door and turned, floor by floor, into the roof over somebody's bed.

## A dome is only as free as the place its glass comes from

On [Day 15](/blog/day-15-inside-the-domes/) we walked inside the domes. Mango trees under glass, bedrooms facing the forest, a theatre sunk into the floor of the master dome.

Every one of those domes is made of triangles. A medium dome needs hundreds of them. A village of 233 people needs thousands, and every one of them is a solar panel that also has to let the light through to the forest underneath.

Buy them, and the village hangs on the same long thread every country hangs on today: a factory far away, a ship, a port, a truck, a price somebody else sets. When the thread breaks, the domes stop growing.

So the village makes them itself.

## The factory is a dome too, the size of the master dome

![The solar factory dome from the forest: the glass shell, five floors of lines inside, the warehouse racks along the bottom.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/outside.jpg)

A hundred and thirty-six metres across, like the master dome at the centre of the village. Inside, five floors stand in rings round a tower. In the tower is one great round lift, wide enough for a crowd of people or a rack of finished glass, and it connects every step of the work from the top to the bottom.

The rule of the building is simple. The smallest things are made at the top. The largest are assembled at the bottom. Everything flows down.

Every floor is laid out the same way. One main line runs round in a loop near the glass. The stations stand on spokes pointing inward from it, like the lines of a star, each machine at the inner end of its spoke. A tray leaves the loop on one lane, rides in to its machine, and comes back out on the other lane with something new on it, then rides on to the next spoke.

![Floor 4 from above: the loop near the glass, the stations on their spokes, the lift in the middle.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/star.jpg)

Where the paths from the four doors meet the loop, the belt runs on underneath and people climb a footbridge over it, so nothing on the line ever has to stop for someone crossing.

![A footbridge over the loop: yellow rails, steps up and down, trays of sand riding underneath.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/footbridge.jpg)

## Floor 4 makes the smallest thing in the dome: the solar cell

![The arc furnace: quartz and carbon go in, silicon comes out at 1900 °C.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/arc-furnace.jpg)

It starts with quartz. An arc furnace melts it with carbon until the oxygen leaves and silicon stays behind. The silicon is refined until only one atom in a million is not silicon.

Then comes the part that still looks like magic to me. In the ingot pullers, a seed crystal is dipped into molten silicon at 1420 °C and drawn up slowly, turning, and one single perfect crystal grows under it.

![Two ingot pullers: tall steel chambers, a glow in each window.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/ingot-pullers.jpg)

Wire saws slice the crystal into wafers thinner than a fingernail. They are etched rough to trap the light, baked in a diffusion furnace at 850 °C to make the junction where electricity is born, coated blue, printed with silver lines, fired, and tested one by one.

![The diffusion furnace, glowing along its length.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/diffusion.jpg)

On the lanes you can watch it happen. Grey wafers ride in, blue cells ride out.

![A spoke on floor 4: grey wafers going in on one lane, coated blue cells coming out on the other.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/lanes-cells.jpg)

What leaves floor 4 on the loop is a tray of finished cells.

## Floor 3 makes everything the cells need to become power

![Cable drums turning as the solar cable winds onto them.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/cable-drums.jpg)

Copper rod comes in coiled. It is drawn down into wire, softened again, and split two ways. Part of it is flattened and tin-plated into the thin ribbons that will join the cells together. The rest gets a jacket of insulation in the cable extruder and winds onto drums as solar cable.

Next to it, the electronics. Plug housings and junction boxes come out of injection moulds. Circuit boards get their solder paste, then their diodes and chips from a pick-and-place machine, then a pass through a reflow oven at 245 °C, then a camera that checks every joint. Board, diodes and cable go into the box.

![The reflow oven on the electronics line.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/reflow.jpg)

Here the tray fills up as it goes round. It starts with a coil of copper. One spoke later there are ribbons beside it, then the coil turns black with insulation, then cut cables, plugs, the empty box, a board, its chips, and at the last spoke the finished junction box with its cables and plugs attached. A robot arm stands behind a yellow fence at every station that needs one.

![A spoke on floor 3: a cable drum and ribbon spools riding in, plug housings added on the way out.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/lanes-cables.jpg)

## Floor 2 makes glass out of sand

This is the floor I was most curious about, because glass feels like something you buy, not something you make.

Sand, soda ash, limestone and dolomite are weighed and mixed in the batch house, and melted at 1550 °C. The melt flows out onto a bath of liquid tin. Glass floats on tin and the tin is perfectly flat, so the glass comes out perfectly flat too. That is how almost every window in the world is made, and here it is how every dome triangle starts.

![The float bath: molten glass floating on liquid tin behind the glowing ports.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/float-bath.jpg)

The ribbon of glass cools slowly in the annealing lehr so it holds no stress. Then it is cut into triangles, its edges ground, washed, tempered at 700 °C and cooled by air until it is strong enough to stand on a dome in a storm, and coated so it reflects less and lets more light through.

![The tempering furnace.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/tempering.jpg)

![A spoke on floor 2: flat float glass going in, cut triangles coming out.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/lanes-glass.jpg)

## Floor 1 puts it together

Everything from above meets here. Cells from floor 4 and ribbons from floor 3 go into the stringers, which solder them into strings. The glass from floor 2 comes down in the lift, gets a film of encapsulant made on this floor from pellets, and a gantry lays the strings onto it with gaps between them, so light falls through to the forest under the dome.

![A stringer, its infrared lamps soldering cells into strings.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/stringer.jpg)

Busbars, a second film, glass on the back as well, a picture taken of every panel in the dark to find any cracked cell while it can still be fixed. Then the laminators: three presses stacked on top of each other, vacuum and 145 °C for a quarter of an hour, and the layers become one piece.

![The laminators, three presses stacked, their lids lifting in turn.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/laminators.jpg)

The junction box goes on last, with its cable.

## The ground floor makes the largest thing in the building: a dome, packed

![The sun simulator: every panel meets a flash of 1000 watts a square metre.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/sun-simulator.jpg)

Aluminium billets are pressed into frame profiles, sealed against the weather, and pressed around each triangle with its gasket. Every panel goes into the dark booth of the sun simulator for one bright flash, which measures exactly how much power it makes. Then an insulation test, a final picture in the dark, a label with its serial number and the dome it belongs to.

In the same hall, steel struts are welded and hubs are milled to their angles. Triangles, struts and hubs are sorted into dome kits, packed onto racks, wrapped, and sent out.

![Dome kits: a gantry sorting triangles for one dome.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/dome-kits.jpg)

![A spoke on the ground floor: framed panels with their struts riding in, a hub added on the way out.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/lanes-kits.jpg)

Around it all stands the warehouse. Three rows of high racks run round the outside of the hall, every pallet place full of what the lines eat and what they make.

![The warehouse aisles, racks of pallets on both sides.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/warehouse.jpg)

Nobody drives in here. The forklifts are driverless, a sensor on top of each mast, and they run the aisles on their own, stop, turn to the rack and lift a pallet into its place. Low robots carry racks of finished glass round the hall and out through the four doors to the yard.

![A driverless forklift in a rack aisle, lifting its pallet.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/forklift.jpg)

## You ride the work down in the lift

![Inside the great lift: a round cabin with glass walls and a rack of finished glass riding along.](/day-16-the-solar-factory-dome/lift-cabin.jpg)

In [Sandbox 3](/games/sandbox-3/) you can walk all five floors. Walk into the lift through one of its four doors, press ↑ and it rises one floor and stops. Press again to go on, or hold the key and it rides through, stopping at every floor on the way. Walk up to the shaft on any floor and it comes to fetch you.

People and humanoid robots work side by side on every floor. Some stand at their machines, some walk the aisles between the lines.

Imagine a morning here. The sun comes through the glass of a dome whose glass was made on the floor below you. A tray of cells goes past on the belt, still warm from the firing furnace. Somewhere under your feet, a forklift lifts a rack of triangles that will be a roof in a new village next month.
