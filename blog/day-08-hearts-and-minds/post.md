Almost every euro in your bank account was born as somebody's debt.

Not metaphorically. Literally.

In 2014 the Bank of England published a paper with the plain title *Money creation in the modern economy*. It said what most of us were never taught at school: most money isn't printed by a central bank. It is created by ordinary commercial banks at the moment they make a loan. Bank deposits made up 97% of the money circulating in the UK at the time.

Your salary, your savings, the price of your rent. All of it sits on a mountain of loans, and every loan has to be paid back with more than was borrowed.

## Money flows from the top down

Picture where new money enters the world.

It starts at the banks. It moves next to whoever can borrow the most — governments, large companies, people who already own property to put up as security. By the time it reaches a wage, a shop or a pension, the people closest to the source have already spent it, invested it and watched prices rise.

Economists have a name for this. The Cantillon effect, after Richard Cantillon, who described it in the 1700s: whoever stands nearest to newly created money gains the most from it.

The system has a direction built into it. Top down. Debt first. Ownership collects at the top, and everybody further down rents access to the rest.

And now a second wave is coming on top of the first.

When AI does more and more of the work, the income people earn from their labour shrinks. What remains is ownership — of the machines, the land, the companies. A money system that concentrates ownership at the top meets a technology that makes ownership the only thing that pays.

That combination does not end well for anyone who only owns their time.

## I spent two and a half years trying to turn it upside down

I was a co-founder of Circles UBI.

You have probably never heard of it, so here is what it was: an open-source project to build a universal basic income from the bottom up. Not paid out by a government, not funded by taxes — created directly by the people using it, on a public blockchain, with nobody in the middle.

It started from an idea so simple it sounds naive, and it is still one of the most beautiful ideas in money I know.

Every human creates their own currency.

No bank. No loan. No debt. You join, and your personal currency starts to flow into your account, steadily, just because you exist. Money that begins as a credit you hold, not a debt someone owes.

Those personal currencies were meant to connect through trust. You trust the people you know, and by trusting someone you accept their currency as equal to yours. Payments would travel along those lines of trust, person to person, across the whole network, without anyone at the centre deciding who is worthy.

A money system drawn from the bottom up.

## It didn't catch on

I have to be honest about that, because the lessons are in the failure.

The trust network did not work. A currency that can only move along personal trust stays as small as the circles of people who already know each other, and asking everyone to decide whose money they will accept, one person at a time, puts the hardest social question right at the front door.

Underneath that sat a bigger problem: a question we never asked clearly enough.

What does anyone actually get from joining?

A currency is only as alive as the things you can do with it. We gave people their own money and a beautiful principle, and then left them standing in an empty market. The early joiners were idealists. Everybody else looked at it, liked the idea, and went back to the euros that paid their rent.

We called it a basic income, too, and that turned out to be the smaller mistake inside the bigger one.

Nobody gets out of bed for *basic*. People don't dream of getting by. They dream of building something, belonging to something, owning a piece of something that matters. Offer survival and you get polite interest. Offer a life a hundred times bigger and people move.

## What Circles was really missing was its other half

It took me a long time after leaving to see it.

Circles' unique gift — one currency for every human — is not weak. It is half of a pair. On its own, a personal currency is a heartbeat with nowhere to send the blood.

So we split money into the two things it was always secretly trying to do.

**Hearts** are the payment currency — the new Circles. Every human mints their own, continuously, without debt. In our design, every citizen starts with 500 hearts of their own and mints 24 more every day, and a gentle demurrage of 7% a year keeps them moving instead of sitting in a drawer.

**Minds** are the ownership currency. Nobody pays with them. You own with them.

A city needs both, and it needs them to stay different.

## Invest your hearts, and they come back as ownership

You cannot buy minds on a market. There is exactly one way to get them: invest your hearts into a cooperative project — a coop — and in return you receive minds, your share of owning it.

Your hearts don't vanish when you do.

The moment they are invested, they become **maiaHearts**: one shared, city-wide currency, steadier than any single person's hearts, held by the coop's treasury to bring its vision to life. Your personal money turns into the city's money, working on something you believe in.

Minds don't buy anything. They show something.

Where people put their hearts is where their attention and conviction are. Minds make that visible — and they compound. The more people believe in a project, the more hearts flow in, the more real it becomes, the more people believe in it.

Imagine an evening in the centre dome of your cluster. Someone stands up with an idea: a bakery that runs on the wheat and fruit from the forest outside. You put fifty hearts in that night, because you want to wake up to that smell. So do forty of your neighbours. Your hearts become maiaHearts in the bakery's treasury, the treasury pays for the ovens — and by spring everyone who believed in it owns a piece of every loaf.

## Ownership is the income after labour

This is where the pair stops being a clever mechanism and becomes the point.

When machines do most of the work, a wage can no longer be how most people share in what gets made. Ownership has to be. The question for the next economy is not how to hand everyone a small cheque so they survive.

It is how every human comes to own part of what their community builds.

Hearts give every person the means to back what they believe in, from the first day, owed to nobody. Minds turn that belief into shared, visible ownership of real things: the bakery, the workshop, the solar roofs, the next dome.

Imagine the day most of the work is done by machines. Nobody panics about their job. The machines belong to the coops, the coops belong to the people who backed them, and the value of everything they make flows back to the hearts that brought it to life.

## This is not the end of capitalism

It is its upgrade.

Capitalism got some things magnificently right. People who take a risk on something new should share in what it becomes. Ownership motivates care. Investment moves resources toward what people believe in.

What it got wrong was who gets to own.

When ownership sits with a small group, the engine that could lift everyone lifts a few. Spread ownership to every human, start everyone with money that is theirs instead of borrowed, and the same engine runs for all of us.

## When the heart and the mind unite

Here is the whole framework in one breath.

Every human mints hearts — their own money, born from their existence, not from debt. Invest them in something shared, and two things happen at once. Your hearts become maiaHearts, the city's common currency, held by the project to build with. And you receive minds: ownership and conviction, compounding around the projects people most want to bring to life.

A heart without a mind gives, and nothing grows.

A mind without a heart plans, and nobody comes.

When the two unite in collective action — millions of people pointing their attention at the same thing they want to exist — that is collective flow, and something close to magic happens. Again and again.

We were never built to survive. Not on a basic income, and not on borrowed money.

We were built to thrive — and to own, together, what we build.

How a single heart becomes a maiaHeart — and why a city needs one currency to pay with and another to own with — is the next story.
