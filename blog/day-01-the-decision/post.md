For years I carried something I didn't dare to say out loud.

Not a secret. A size.

The dream itself was never the problem. I could see it with my eyes closed — clear, alive, obviously possible. The problem was the person holding it. Me. Four startups that didn't make it. A long list of reasons to get a normal job and a normal dream in a normal size.

So it went where those dreams go. Notes. Sketches. A folder that gets opened on a Sunday evening and closed again before anyone can see it.

## A drawer never keeps a dream the same size

That's the part nobody warns you about.

You think you're keeping it safe. You're not. It keeps growing in there, quietly, and every year it gets heavier to lift out. The fear grows with it. The longer you wait, the more a first step feels like a public confession: *this is what I actually want, and I might fail at it in front of everyone.*

Privately, a plan stays perfect. Out loud, it has wrong numbers in it, and somebody will point at the wrong numbers.

So I didn't say it.

I told myself I was waiting for the right time. I was waiting to feel like the kind of person who gets to dare something this big.

That feeling never comes by waiting for it.

## The question I kept asking was the wrong one

*Who am I to build this?*

I asked it for years, and there is no good answer to it, because it's built to keep you small. Every version of it starts with *I* — my track record, my failures, my limits — and measured against those, any dream worth having looks impossible.

The turn came when I stopped measuring the dream against myself.

## Nobody builds anything big alone

Look at what humans do when they move in the same direction.

More than six million English Wikipedia articles, written by volunteers nobody paid. Cathedrals raised over generations by people who knew they'd never see the finished roof. Open-source software that quietly runs most of the internet, built by strangers who never met.

None of that came from one brave person. It came from people pointing their attention at the same thing, together — and discovering they were capable of far more than anyone had ever allowed them to believe.

That's when it landed.

The dream was never too big. It was just never meant to be carried by one person.

Not *I build a city.*

*We* build a city.

## We were never built to survive

For most of history, the blueprint handed to us was survival. Get through the month. Compete for what's left. Keep your head down and your dreams in a normal size.

That blueprint is running out.

And what comes after it isn't scarcity. It's an age of abundance that's already arriving — when people stop competing for scraps and start building together, owning together, in flow together.

Imagine waking up and the work in front of you is the thing you would do even if nobody paid you. Not a job you survive. Something you're building with people you'd choose.

Imagine a million people pointing their attention at the same thing at once. The same flow you feel on your best day, working alone — multiplied by everyone around you, every day.

We are far more capable than we have ever been allowed to believe.

The only ceiling is imagination.

Not 100x more stuff.

100x more life.

## So this is day one

What changed wasn't the conviction. The dream never wobbled.

What changed is who's carrying it.

From today it gets built in public, messy, in front of everyone — the game first, then the real thing. The numbers that turn out wrong, and the ones that survive. A city that grows from one founder to a million co-founders, every one of them building and owning what they help create.

Imagine the first morning a founder you've never met reads this, closes their own drawer, and says out loud the thing they've been carrying for years.

That's the moment it stops being my dream.

The city doesn't exist yet. The journal does, and so does the *we*.

When we unite in vision, the impossible becomes possible.
