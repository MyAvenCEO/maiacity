## The story

Picture a call center of a hundred trained people. They find warm prospects, phone them at the perfect moment and introduce your business, around the clock, for free. All they ask for is one script a day.

Most businesses already have one and leave it idle.

To see why, go back to 2007. Facebook was taking off with the social graph: you saw what your friends posted, and they saw what you posted. Promoting your business every day meant boring your cousin and your friend from high school, and within a week they were sick of you.

The interest graph came next. The feed went looking for the most interesting things on the planet and showed them to everyone. It didn't know what a video was about, only that people stopped, liked, commented and shared. So a man in Texas setting fire to a Ferrari in a cornfield beat a quiet expert every time. People did ridiculous things for attention. Some got millions of followers and no money.

This year the engine changed again. It now runs on AI that understands what a post says and who it is for. A video too dull for the world but useful to dentists gets shown to the dentists. A brand-new account with hardly any followers can reach a million people if its post is for a million people. Or it can reach exactly 5,000 people, if they are 5,000 heads of software companies.

The feed has become a matchmaker. That is the call center.

It still needs a script it can read. The AI looks at your last 50 posts and decides who you are and whom to show you to. Posts that wander from topic to topic leave it guessing, and garbage in is still garbage out.

So every post does two things. It names the audience, out loud, in its first line. And it says what you do for them: the problem you solve, the result you deliver, the obstacle you remove. Then you post again tomorrow, and again, about the same thing. It feels repetitive to you. To the machine and to the people it finds, it's a clear signal.

Consistency pays twice. People notice you for the first time around the eleventh time they see you, and a clear account gets put in front of them again and again. Every post also keeps a long tail: a few thousand views on its first day, a few thousand more in the months after. A hundred posts that each draw a few thousand views a month add up to 100,000 views a month from the back catalog alone.

You know it's working when 80 to 90% of the people who see each new post have never seen you before.

No Ferrari, no stunt, no follower count required. Say who it's for and what it does for them, every day, and the feed finds them.

## Beliefs that shift

- *Reach comes from followers you already have.* → The AI shows a post to the people it's for, so a new account with a clear post can reach a million people, or exactly the 5,000 who matter.
- *To be seen you need to be outrageous.* → Outrage was the interest graph's game. The semantic feed rewards a post that clearly names its audience and its use.
- *Posting the same message every day is annoying.* → It reads as a signal: people take you in around the eleventh sighting, and the machine learns whom to find.

## What we learn

- **Name the audience in the first line of every post.** Our journal days and their derivatives open on a hook about the work. Saying who it is for, such as the people who want to co-found a city of a million, gives the feed the label it needs.
- **Pair the audience with an outcome.** "What we do for you" is the second half of the label. For maiaCITY it could be a place to live, own and build together. Each day's article and every post derived from it should repeat it in its own words.
- **The last 50 posts are your profile.** In 19 days the journal has covered domes, food forests, films, avenDB and trading avens. A wide range reads as range to a person, but the machine only reads it as a blur. A small set of fixed topics, each with its own audience, makes the back catalog speak with one voice.
- **Daily is the rhythm we already have.** The content board publishes a journal day and its derivatives every day. What the video adds is aim: the same audience and the same promise in every one.
- **The back catalog is an asset.** Every day page, Short and post keeps drawing views long after its launch. The 19 days so far are a library to link back to and re-cut, not old news.
- **Measure new viewers.** His test is 80 to 90% new viewers on each post. The platforms' analytics show that number, so we can track it per day.
- **The same rule fits the avens.** A trading aven that broadcasts clear, consistent intentions gets matched faster on an order book. A world where avens advertise who they want to trade with is worth a test.

## Open questions

- The video never names its maker, and YouTube is blocked from here. The style and the closing offer of a "game plan session" suggest a business-coaching channel. Check the channel name before adding an author.
- Claims to check: that the "semantic era" started this year, that people notice you on the eleventh sighting, and that 80 to 90% new viewers is the right target. The video gives no sources for them.
- Who exactly is maiaCITY's intended audience: future residents, co-founders and investors, or builders and makers? A few clearly named audiences would fit the journal's mix better than one vague one.
- The end of the video is a sales pitch for coaching calls. The method doesn't depend on it.
