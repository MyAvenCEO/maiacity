Note: a short broadcast segment with several unnamed speakers — family of the founders, school
staff, and the architect who has developed the campus buildings. What follows are the points
the report card is built on, in the order they are made.

---

**The school.** Green School Bali was created by the speaker's father and stepmother. The
position stated: education needs to change and adapt to the future; students there learn to
make the world sustainable; the purpose of education should be to make the world a better
place.

**Heart of School.** Described as the favourite building on campus — many parts, many angles,
"exciting, mad, and fun", and a beautiful place to be.

**Open to the air.** Being wireless and open connects the school to the natural environment.
It is also acknowledged as distracting — and the answer given is that it is okay to get
distracted by nature.

**Learning by building.** Middle-school students started their own kitchen and designed a
whole restaurant system — waiters, chefs, managers, accountants — providing a food service for
the rest of the school as part of their learning. Staff contrast what students say at home:
not "I read a really cool book" but "we built a new place for the chickens". Those are
described as the real experiences and real memories the school wants to create.

**The architect.** Returned to Bali twelve years ago after walking into Heart of School and
wanting to be part of it. Since then, each new building on campus has been developed by them
and their team.

**The Green School Arc.** The latest building, a sports space. The design intent: a space where
your whole body wants to throw a ball the farthest and highest arc you could. The practical
problem: spanning that space with no columns in between. Described as one of the most
beautiful gyms in the world — massive, intricately designed, sustainable bamboo.

**Bamboo, before.** Long understood across the tropics as a short-term, humble material useful
for simple things in village life. You could not count on it for long-lasting structures
without a proper treatment solution, because untreated bamboo is destroyed by insect attack.

**Bamboo, after.** For almost two decades there has been a way to treat bamboo naturally: it is
made salty enough that the bugs stop eating it. Treated bamboo buildings can last as long as
wooden buildings. With further treatment technology and evolving design ingenuity, the
expectation is buildings that last hundreds of years.

**Both ends of the market.** The same material can make a gorgeous, glamorous, luxurious home
and can serve for relief housing and other basic necessities — the latter described as much
more important.

**Why beauty.** The structures around Green School are a promise to the kids that a beautiful,
sustainable world is possible. Beauty is relevant because we are emotionally compelled by a
sense of wonder when we walk into a beautiful space.
