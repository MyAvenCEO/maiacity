## The story

There are more than four times as many livestock on Earth as people. Farmed cattle alone weigh nearly ten times as much as every wild mammal put together.

So TED-Ed invents a wizard. One wave of the wand and all meat is gone from the shelves, along with the wish to eat it, and the animals raised for food are whisked off to another planet.

Overnight, food-related greenhouse gas emissions fall by about 63%.

The rest of the morning goes badly. Gone with the animals are some 70 billion chickens, 1.5 billion pigs, 300 million cattle and 200 million tonnes of fish and shellfish a year, and everyone turns to fruit, vegetables and legumes at once. Most dietitians agree that diet can carry a healthy life. There simply isn't enough of it yet, and produce prices soar.

In Mongolia, where little grows, people are left with almost nothing to eat. Salmon-eating tribes of the Pacific Northwest lose food, work and part of their religion in one stroke. Tens of millions of anglers lose a living that dwindling fish stocks were already taking from them, and households across the developing world lose the income their animals gave them.

That is the cost of doing it by magic. A food system cannot be switched; it has to be regrown.

And it does regrow. Farmers move into crops, and their neighbours stop breathing the air around livestock barns. As fields expand, prices come back down, until eating vegetarian is cheaper than eating meat in most countries. No forest has to be cleared for any of it, because the land that grew animal feed is free now, and the new diets need less land and less water in total.

Millions of deaths a year are avoided, partly through less heart disease and cancer linked to red meat. No new pathogens from hunted wild animals, no new flu from farmed pigs, no superbugs bred in cattle fed antibiotics in advance.

Then the years start to show. Amazonian birds have more forest to fly over. Fewer cheetahs are shot for coming too close to a herd. Bees, wasps and butterflies thrive as wild land spreads, and the crops they pollinate yield more. The oceans refill.

Over millennia, bodies may follow. People in traditionally vegetarian regions already carry a mutation for processing plant fats, and we might lose others, like the knack for pulling iron out of meat.

The wizard is not coming, of course. Meat eating is still rising worldwide, and even if fossil fuels stopped tomorrow, food as usual plus a growing population would carry the planet past 1.5 °C by 2100.

Except the problem was never all meat equally. Beef and dairy make over 60% of food emissions and supply about 18% of the world's calories. A diet with modest amounts of chicken often emits less than a vegetarian diet heavy in cheese and milk.

So the lever is much smaller than a wand. Eat less beef, cheese and milk, and most of the meatless world arrives anyway.

## Beliefs that shift

- *Eating for the climate means going fully vegetarian.* → Beef and dairy carry most of the weight. Modest chicken can beat a cheese-heavy vegetarian plate.
- *There isn't enough land to feed everyone on plants.* → Most farmland grows feed for animals. Without them, plant diets need less land and water, with no new clearing.
- *Ending meat is a sacrifice with nothing back.* → It returns health, fewer pandemics, more wild land, more pollinators and fuller oceans, but people who live from animals need a way through it.

## What we learn

- **Aim at the cow, not at meat.** Beef and dairy give over 60% of food emissions for about 18% of calories. That is the line to cut first.
- **Change must be grown, not switched.** The overnight version brings price spikes, hunger where little grows and lost livelihoods. Supply has to be planted ahead of demand.
- **Feed land is the reserve.** The land that feeds animals is where a plant-first diet grows, so no forest has to fall.
- **Plants first, small animals second.** This is the shape of maiaCITY's food forests: fruit, nuts, beans, greens and roots in seven layers, with hens in a run and fish in the dome's pond, and no cattle anywhere.
- **Pollinators pay twice.** Wild land that comes back raises yields of the crops bees and butterflies pollinate. Every forest hex keeps its pond, its deadwood and its flowering layers for them.
- **Respect the places and cultures built on animals.** Herders, anglers and salmon peoples lose most in the sudden version. A transition should give them a path, not a verdict.
