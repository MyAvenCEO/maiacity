## The story

The colour palette started under a microscope.

The designers at Geoship took reddish soil from parts of the world they loved, looked at it up close, and pulled every colour out of it — soft peaches, terracotta clay, sand. Then they scattered those colours through the inside of a dome, so it would feel like living inside the earth.

The dome is called Āman. Its designers and engineers work so close together that every drawing turns into a conversation: not only what they want to build, but what they can.

The problem they are working against is old. Most homes are boxes, built the standard way, and anything else — earth building, natural materials, round structures — gets filed as a niche. Something made by hand, in small batches, for the few people willing to live outside the norm.

Geoship wants the opposite. Their stated goal is to make natural homes mass producible: homes made from things that come from nature without harming it, and can go back to nature without harming it.

That goal meets resistance at every scale.

The central staircase was not an easy feat, and they built it anyway, because it was worth it. The layout had to follow the human day — waking up, cooking, washing your hands — so that moving through the home feels easy, not designed.

Healthy materials were harder still. Every tile in the shower, the furniture, the cabinets, the counters and the floor had to come from partners whose sourcing they trusted. Even the paint had to have zero VOCs, so it isn't releasing chemicals into the air — a detail most people never think about, in a home that is mostly covered in paint.

And a home like this can't appear all at once. It comes together layer after layer, system after system, until it finally stands in one space: soft light, a gentle enclosure, a big skylight that brings clarity into the room.

What they end up describing is not a shelter. You have your skin, you have your clothes, and then you have your home — the next layer that is yours.

Their closing question is the whole idea in one line: why are things the way they are? If we made it, we can change it.

## Beliefs that shift

- *Natural and earth-built homes are a handmade niche.* → They can be designed from the start to be produced for many people.
- *A home is a shelter from rain and cold.* → A home is a third skin, and it should be as healthy as what you wear.
- *Design starts with how a house looks.* → The layout comes from the human day; the look comes from nature.

## What we learn

- **Form follows function, and the function is a day.** Plan from waking up, cooking and washing, and the layout stops getting in the way.
- **Take the palette from the land.** Colours pulled from local soil make a building feel like it belongs to its place.
- **Health is in the invisible materials.** Paint covers most of a home; choosing zero-VOC paint is one of the fastest ways to change the air you breathe.
- **Source through trusted partners.** Tiles, floors, counters and furniture are all choices about what enters the home.
- **Design and engineering belong in the same room.** The best result sits between what you imagine and what can be built.
- **Question the status quo out loud.** Everything built by people can be rebuilt by people.
