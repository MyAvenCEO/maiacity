# Restart snapshot encoding outside the shared Lab lock

The saver formerly called `Lab::warming` under the shared Lab mutex. That serialized replay states and key lists, hashed IDs, and encrypted both restart summaries while local operations waited. Disk I/O was outside the lock, but this CPU work was not.

`Lab::capture_warm` now captures owned inputs and the existing receipt tokens. The node releases Lab before encoding and sealing them. The canonical view writer is shared by ordinary `Replay::to_view` and owned `ViewCapture`; the stored format and engine binding are unchanged. `Lab::warming` remains a compatibility wrapper for direct callers.

Capture copies ordered IDs, standing bits, checkpoint actor pairs, tail positions, and key handles. States share their existing Arc-backed payloads. It does not copy edit bodies, parent/depth/covered indexes, or serialize anything. This is O(history IDs + actor pairs + key count), not constant work. It may still allocate under Lab; retaining shared States can also make later mutations copy collections through existing copy-on-write behavior. No sub-50 ms claim is made.

Each capture draws a new secret seed under Lab and owns a domain-separated zeroizing RNG. Plaintext key and view buffers are zeroized after sealing. Existing shelf-key, engine, prefix validation, and `warmed` store-revision/lineage fences remain unchanged. Append can preserve a captured prefix; restore, lock, withdrawal and competing acknowledgement retain their existing rejection behavior. Dropping a capture acknowledges nothing. An already admitted encoder may finish after a concurrent lock; its private key copies survive until it completes or drops, then zeroize. Its output is encrypted, and the lock-invalidated receipt cannot acknowledge the new store state. This does not promise immediate erasure of all in-flight work at the instant of lock.

The save lane/book lock and the admitted producer still span actual encoding and persistence. The native blocking closure releases Lab before encoding. Cancelling its waiter cannot cancel a running producer or acknowledge its receipt. On WASM the encoder still runs synchronously on the node worker: this change alone does not make worker CPU work preemptible or solve browser responsiveness.

Lean `WarmCapture.lean` records ownership and receipt-ordering obligations; it is not a scheduler, cryptographic implementation, or latency proof. Native regressions cover captured byte parity, append/restore/lock while encoding is delayed, dropped capture, and a held encoder with a concurrent local write and cancelled waiter. Existing warming, warm cap-group, snapshot acknowledgement and shutdown tests remain relevant.
