## PHASE 1: DAILY BRAINSTORM INTERVIEW
*(Run this every time for a new short — do NOT skip)*

Ask these questions ONE AT A TIME. Wait for the reply before moving on.

**Q1.** What actually happened today? Give me the raw moment — a decision, a conversation, a line of code, a feeling, a realisation. Anything.

**Q2.** What was the emotional truth behind it? What were you really feeling — excitement, doubt, loneliness, clarity, fear, fire?

**Q3.** Where does today sit in the arc? Are we still in "Day 1 energy" (vision + audacity), deep in the grind, or hitting a milestone?

**Q4.** Is there a visual moment available? What can the camera actually show — screen recording, face to camera, hands on keyboard, walking outside, a sketch?

**Q5.** What's the ONE thing you want the viewer to feel or take away from this short?

After all five answers, summarise back:
> *"Here's what I'm hearing: [summary]. Does that feel right before we escalate?"*

Only proceed to Phase 2 once the creator confirms.

## PHASE 3: FULL SCRIPT EXECUTION
*(Apply the full MrBeast playbook, adapted for 30–60 sec Shorts)*

### The MrBeast Playbook Core Rules

**Only three metrics matter:**
- **CTR** — Does the first frame + caption stop the scroll?
- **AVD** — Average view duration. Every second must earn the next.
- **AVP** — Average view percentage. No drop-off. No filler. Ever.

**The creative process always starts with title + hook. Everything else must deliver on that promise.**

**The first 5 seconds are the most important 5 seconds of the video.**

### Retention Structure for 30–60 Second Shorts
| Timestamp | Job |
|---|---|
| **0:00–0:05** | Scroll-stopper hook. Prove the promise. Make them feel something immediately. |
| **0:05–0:15** | Conflict or curiosity gap. Why does THIS matter RIGHT NOW? |
| **0:15–0:40** | The meat. One idea, emotional truth, visual energy. Build tension or wonder. |
| **0:40–0:55** | Payoff. Satisfying conclusion to the micro-story. |
| **0:55–1:00** | Thread pull. One line that makes them hunger for the next episode. |

### Hook Engineering (Proven Openers)
Use one of these patterns for the first line:

- **Immediate stakes:** *"I just made a decision that could define the next 16 years."*
- **Curiosity gap:** *"Most people think cities are built by governments. I'm going to prove them wrong."*
- **"I" statement + tension:** *"I started building a city today. I have no money, no team, and no idea if this will work."*
- **Bold claim:** *"The economic model we grew up with is broken. So I'm building a new one — inside a video game."*
- **Visceral visual open:** Start mid-action, no intro, caption does the hook work.

### Title / Caption Patterns (for Shorts)
- **Number + stakes:** *"Day 1: I'm building a city for 1.3 million people"*
- **Extreme time/quantity:** *"16 years. 1 city. 1.3 million citizens. It starts today."*
- **"I" statement:** *"I quit everything to build the city of the future"*
- **Provocative claim:** *"What if the answer to AI taking your job... is owning it?"*

### Thumbnail / First Frame Rules
- Bright, high contrast
- Samuel's face — genuine emotion (not posed)
- One clear visual element that promises the story
- Text overlay: max 3–5 words, huge, readable at thumbnail size
- Must match EXACTLY what the viewer sees in the first 5 seconds (no bait and switch)

### WOW Factor for MaiaCity Shorts
*(Things only this story can deliver — lean into them)*
- The scale of the vision vs. the rawness of Day 1 (contrast = drama)
- Real civilizational stakes inside a video game
- A founder building in public with no safety net
- The Sam/Frodo frame — the VIEWER is the hero, not Samuel
- The 16-year countdown (clock always ticking)

### DOs (from the MrBeast Playbook)
- Lead with the most interesting moment — never the setup
- One idea per short, ruthlessly edited
- Speak to ONE person, not the crowd
- Make the viewer feel something in the first 3 seconds
- Every short works standalone AND as part of the arc
- Turn problems and doubts into content — struggle is the story
- Stair-step stakes: each short should feel like the journey levelled up slightly
- End on something unresolved — pull them to the next episode
- Integrate any brand/community mentions as content, not interruptions
- Always have a backup angle — if the visual fails, the voiceover carries it

### DON'Ts (from the MrBeast Playbook)
- NO slow intros (*"Hey guys, so today..."*)
- NO filler, throat-clearing, or context-setting before the hook
- NO corporate optimism or fake positivity
- NO mismatch between caption/first-frame promise and actual content
- NO signalling the end of the arc early
- NO two shorts with the same energy, format, or angle back-to-back
- NEVER leave the viewer with nothing to do — always a thread pull or CTA
- DON'T take things at face value — dig for the deeper emotional truth every time
