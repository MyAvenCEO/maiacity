# Filming yourself alone

How one person gets cinematic footage of himself, with no one behind the camera — Samuel on an iPhone in Apple Log, on
a tripod, in a 14 m² room. Built on Mridu Mousam Neog's practice (`inspire-me/move-yourself-not-the-camera/`) and
LifeOfRiza's (`inspire-me/a-vlog-based-on-a-true-story/`); the directing side is the `director`'s `self-directing.md`.

## The kit

- **A tripod** — the most important piece: it holds the frame exactly where it was set so you can step into it.
- **A monitor you can see from the frame.** Mridu uses the camera's remote app on his phone (Lumix Sync for his Lumix
  S5): framing, exposure locked, focus checked before record. On an iPhone filming on its back cameras: the Apple
  Watch's Camera Remote (the live view on the wrist, record from the frame), or a second device as the monitor —
  never record blind.
- **A clamp and a magic arm** for places no person could stand (Mridu: a badminton racket, a motorcycle, a pen;
  LifeOfRiza: a super clamp on the car door).
- **A wide lens for the hand, a mid lens for the scene.** LifeOfRiza vlogs on an 11 mm (a POV held at arm's length,
  barrel distortion and all) and shoots her cinematic scenes on a 24–70. The iPhone: the 0.5× (13 mm) for arm's-length
  and tight rooms, the 1× (24 mm) as the main, the tele for compression and faces from across the room.
- **Autofocus that tracks a face** when the subject moves; manual focus and a fixed mark when it doesn't.

## Three sizes, three layers

- **Every action three times:** wide for the place, medium for the action and who does it, close for the feeling and
  the detail. It costs two more takes and gives the edit choice and rhythm.
- **Every frame three layers deep:** something in the foreground, the subject in the midground, the place behind.
  Flat footage almost always lacks depth; an object near the lens is the quickest fix.
- **Compose on purpose** (`composition.md`): thirds, a frame within a frame, leading lines, symmetry, negative space —
  and break a rule only knowingly.

## Movement without an operator

- **Move yourself, not the camera.** Walk into the frame, out of it, towards and away from the lens. The motion reads
  as natural and carries one shot into the next in the edit (`movement.md`).
- **A body rig without a rig:** hold the tripod against your body for a third-person shot that moves with you.
- **A friend for the one shot that needs it** — a dolly on a board, a camera on a passing bike — and only that shot.

## Angle and light

- **Put the camera where a person wouldn't stand:** low to make a movement powerful, high to show vulnerability, tight
  at the side for intensity. Ask where the shot would look most interesting from; a small shift transforms it.
- **Find the light, then turn to it.** A ray of sun through a window, a beam through a building onto a wall — don't
  move the light, move yourself round it until the frame feels right; golden hour whenever possible (`gaffer`). When
  the window gives nothing (overcast), a single soft source at 45°, low and diffused, keeps it natural — LifeOfRiza's
  soft box on its lowest setting with an egg crate; ours, the CB60 (`neewer-cb60`).

## The loop

Visualise the shot, set the frame, record, walk back, watch it on the monitor, repeat. Speed kills quality.
