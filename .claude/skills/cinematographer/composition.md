# Composition: what the frame says

## The visual components (Bruce Block, *The Visual Story*)

Every frame is made of seven components: **space, line, shape, tone, colour, movement, rhythm**. The opening of a film
teaches the audience what each means; after that, the rules bind every shot and every department.

- **Contrast raises intensity, affinity lowers it.** Deep against flat, diagonal against horizontal, bright against
  dark, warm against cold, moving against still, fast against slow.
- **Graph the story's intensity** over time and plan each component under it — as a constant, a progression, or a
  contrast/affinity pattern. Spend the biggest contrast on the climax; resolve with affinity.
- Deakins's palette in *The Shawshank Redemption* warms as hope grows; Morrison roughens *Mudbound*'s palette and camera
  once the story reaches the farm.

## Depth

Block's depth cues: perspective, size difference, movement (the foreground passes faster than the background:
parallax), diffusion, tonal and colour separation, up/down position in frame, overlap, focus. Deep space reads as more
intense than flat space. Overlap figures and never line them up at equal distances; separate the planes with light
(Mascelli). Ours: three layers in every frame — something near, the subject, the place (`solo.md`).

## Composition patterns

| Pattern | When | Carries |
|---|---|---|
| Eyes on the upper third, normal headroom | the default single | ease |
| Centre, symmetry | ritual, authority, confrontation, a person talking to the lens | order, control (Kubrick, Anderson, Fraser) |
| Frame within a frame (doors, windows, glass) | isolation, entrapment, watching, a threshold | confinement (*Empire of Light*) |
| Converging lines, a one-point corridor | leading the eye | pull, dread (*The Shining*) |
| Diagonals against verticals and horizontals | rising conflict against strength and rest | stress against stability |
| Foreground, midground, background | a person in their place | immersion (Lubezki, Block) |
| Flat space (frontal planes, no overlap) | trapped or stylised worlds | containment (Anderson) |
| Deep space | scale, complexity, danger | intensity |
| One deep shot in a flat film | an entrance, a revelation | shock |
| Negative space, heavy headroom | loneliness, pressure | weight (*Mr. Robot*) |
| Lead room / look room | normal gaze and movement | freedom |
| Short-siding (the gaze towards the near edge) | doubt, a blocked character | unease |
| Formal against informal balance | ceremony against action | rest against tension (Mascelli) |
| A triangle of heads or objects | groups | stability |
| Low / high / dutch | power / vulnerability / disturbance | Mascelli |
| Overhead | a map, a pattern | detachment |
| Deep against selective focus | the viewer chooses against attention forced | realism against subjectivity |

**Space describes the person:** Mendes and Deakins framed their lonely heroine in big spaces, behind glass, inside a
booth. Our room: the window and the door are the frames within the frame; the dome avenues give one-point
perspective; height gives status.

## Gaze and framing a person

- Talking to the lens: frame centred (it also survives the 9:16 crop).
- Looking off-lens to think: give look room on the side of the gaze. Short-side only for doubt or pressure.
- The lens at eye height — about 1.6 m standing, lower seated. A low-held phone makes an angle you didn't choose.

## 16:9 and 9:16

- A 9:16 centre crop of a 16:9 frame keeps 31.6 % of its width; on the vertical's long side a 24 mm then looks like a
  ~43 mm. Keep face and hands in the centre third, or shoot vertical natively when the vertical cut leads.
- **Instagram Reels safe area** (Meta's ad guide): keep the top 14 %, the bottom 35 % and 6 % on each side free of
  anything that matters — on 1080 × 1920, a safe box of about 950 × 979 px. YouTube Shorts' zones: unverified, keep the
  same box.
- In the worlds, each shape has its own framing (pitch, dy): put the eyes near the upper third inside the safe box, and
  fill the extra height with Block's up/down cue — near things low in frame, far things high.
- Fraser framed *Dune* for the biggest format first and protected the smaller ones; we compose for 16:9 and check 9:16
  every time.

## Mistakes the pros avoid

- Figures at equal distances in flat light (Mascelli).
- The same size and angle twice in a row, or hodge-podge coverage (Mascelli).
- Crossing the line of action without a bridging shot (Mascelli; `director`, `staging.md`).
- Flare and lens gimmicks; colour and complexity for their own sake (Deakins).
- Framing only for the smallest screen (Fraser).
