# Story producer

One producer, one crew. Every story maiaCITY tells — a journal day, a 90-second reel, a ten-minute film, a title
card — runs through the same laws and the same crew. The producer keeps the laws, the order and the pipeline; each
craft lives in its own skill, loaded when the work reaches it.

## The four laws (never break them)

1. **The hook goes as extreme as the truth allows.** The biggest, strangest end state the facts can carry, set
   against the reader's biggest belief. A mild hook is a lost reader. → `hook-writer`
2. **One curiosity loop, open from the first line to the last.** A single arching question the viewer cannot leave
   without the answer to — held open by progress, conflict and doubt, answered in the last lines, fast. →
   `storyteller` (`arc.md`, `retention.md`)
3. **The biggest possible transformation for the viewer.** Decide *from* (what they believe arriving) and *to* (what
   they leave with) before a word is written or a frame is planned. → `storyteller` (`arc.md`)
4. **Lead with the pain.** Every intro shows what is wrong in the viewer's own life before the new world. Pain,
   never dread. → `storyteller` (`arc.md`)

And always: **every fact true** (nothing invented about Samuel's life, the viewers, attempts or results — a film may
be *based on* a true story, re-enacted, never made up), **full conviction** (a vision being built, not hoped for),
**contrast on every axis** (a high only reads against a low), and **every scene a step forward** (`storyteller`,
`scenes.md`).

## The crew — load the skill the work needs

| Skill | The role | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| `storyteller` | the writer | the transformation, the arching question, three acts scaled to a short film, scenes that move the story forward, tension and release, retention, the journal voice, voice-over |
| `hook-writer` | the hook writer | titles, first lines, rehooks, title cards and thumbnails, a film's first seconds |
| `director` | the director | what each scene is about, the shots for each line, the shot list and storyboard, staging and continuity, directing yourself on camera |
| `cinematographer` | the director of photography | composition, shot sizes and angles, lenses, camera movement, exposure for log, the virtual camera of the worlds, filming yourself alone |
| `gaffer` | the light | motivated light, ratios, windows and practicals, colour temperature, time of day — in the room and in the worlds |
| `neewer-cb60` | one fixture | the Neewer CB60 RGB: its controls, modes and recipes, and its twin in the room |
| `production-designer` | worlds, sets, things | the worlds and their sets, the 3D models library, art direction |
| `editor` | the cut | cutting on the word, the transitions, pacing and its alternatives, the process, the audit before a render |
| `colorist` | the grade | the base correction, then the look; colour across the arc; rendered shots beside camera footage |
| `sound-designer` | the sound | voice takes, the score, beds and hits, silence, the mix |
| `content-derivatives` | distribution | a finished day's posts per platform, from its base article |

## The order of work

Story before production, always.

1. **Story** (`storyteller`): the transformation, the arching question, the arc as beats with their intensity. Show
   Samuel before drafting.
2. **Hooks** (`hook-writer`): at least ten, annotated, one kept for the first line, one for the title, one for the
   title card.
3. **Draft** (`storyteller`): the post, or the film's script — scene by scene, each scene a step.
4. **Pre-production** — the film planned before a frame is taken:
   - `director`: what each scene is about, the shots per line, the shot list and storyboard.
   - `production-designer`: the world and its set, the props, the art direction.
   - `cinematographer`: frames, lenses, moves, exposure. `gaffer`: the light of every scene.
5. **Production**: the voice takes (`sound-designer`), the footage (`cinematographer`, `director` — Samuel films
   himself), the world shots as shot records.
6. **Post** — like a real post house: `editor` cuts and locks the edit, `colorist` balances and then builds the look,
   `sound-designer` scores, designs and mixes.
7. **Audit and render**: the editor's audit (`editor`, `audit.md`), then the Mac app renders (`pipeline.md`).
8. **Derivatives** (`content-derivatives`).
