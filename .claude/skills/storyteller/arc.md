# Story arc and transformation

## One transformation — the biggest the truth allows

Before a word is written, decide the single shift the piece delivers:

```
from: <the belief the reader arrives with>
to:   <what they leave with>
```

Make the distance **as large as possible**. The reader should leave seeing their own life differently, not just
knowing a fact. A small shift ("domes are efficient") produces a small story; a large one ("a good life is delivered
to us" → "a good life grows where we live") carries everything else.

- Day 19: *a good life depends on things brought from far away* → *a good life grows where you live, built by the
  people who live there*.
- The last lines land the *to* in something concrete — a unit, a place, a thing that can be built — never an
  abstraction.
- maiaCITY's register is **vision**: self-sufficiency, health, nature, well-being, the thriving life. Not fear, not
  crisis, not safety. Lows show how we live now against how we could; they never sell dread.

## The arching question — one curiosity loop to the end

Every piece has **one question it answers**, opened in the first line and held open until the last (Jenny Hoyos).
It is the spine: every section moves toward its answer, and the reader cannot leave without it.

- It is concrete and answerable — best with an answer the reader cannot guess (a number, a place, a thing).
- It is stated in the first line (or is the title), never after a setup.
- It stays open: no section may answer it early. Smaller questions are opened and closed along the way; the big one
  closes only at the end.
- When it closes, **end fast**. The reader came for the answer, not a wrap-up. The last line is the door to tomorrow,
  never a recap.

## Lead with the pain

Every intro educates first: **what is wrong**, before any of the vision. Right after the hook, name the pain in the
reader's own life — concrete, felt, specific (*the street outside your door was laid for engines, not for your
children*; *your food grew in fields you will never see*). Two to four lines, then one line that makes it visible
(*we have lived inside this so long that we stopped seeing it*). Only then the new world.

- The pain is the reader's, today — never ours, never abstract ("cities are inefficient").
- It is the *from* of the transformation made tangible: the reader must feel the wrong before the right can land.
- Pain is not dread. No doom, no crisis, no fear-selling: it is how we live now, seen clearly, against how we could.
- Every section repeats the move in small: the old way first (a short, sharp low), then the new (the high).

## Hook → story → lessons (what it means for you)

The shape that keeps working for small channels and big ones alike, in three parts — all three are needed:

1. **The hook** — anchor and promise (the `hook-writer` skill).
2. **The story** — a real transformation with a clear before and after, told personally: what Samuel believed, what
   he saw, what changed. Specific, honest details build the trust that makes the rest believable; they are also the
   one thing no one else (and no AI) can copy. Small is fine — a mindset shift, a failure, a first step. Only what
   really happened (`writing.md`).
3. **The lessons** — zoom out: *what does this mean for the reader?* Not "here is what happened to me" but "here is what
   it means for you" — what they can see, want or do differently in their own life. This is where the piece becomes
   worth their time, and where the transformation (from → to) lands on the reader.

In a journal post the story and the lessons interleave — each piece of the answer is found in the story and turned
toward the reader in the next lines. In a film the lessons are the payoff line and the last images.

**Promise the whole.** Tell the reader the payoff needs all of it ("three pieces; you need all three") — leaving early
then feels like leaving with nothing.

## Show progress — the pieces

A question alone does not hold anyone for long. The reader must **feel each step bring the answer closer** — Jenny's
tomatoes, then lettuce, then cheese, so leaving feels like stopping halfway.

- Break the answer into pieces the reader can count (food → water → power → the home → the neighbours → the number).
- Each piece is visibly one step closer; say so when it helps ("Three pieces down.").
- Pay off in pieces: a small answer that raises a bigger question, then answer that.

## Add conflict — the obstacles

Progress alone is smooth sailing, and smooth sailing bores. Every piece meets resistance:

- **The obvious answer fails first.** The bigger, grander version is almost always the first obstacle (one enormous
  dome for 5,000 → the apartment block again, only rounder).
- **A false summit** — an answer that looks final and breaks — is the strongest tension spike before the real peak.
- A B-plot the reader also cares about (Jenny's sceptical mother) doubles the stake.

## Keep the answer uncertain

Tension is not knowing. Right up to the end the answer must feel as if it might not come — the last obstacle comes
late, close to the answer.

## The machine (reference, never visible)

```
HOOK → CONTEXT → PROBLEM → INTENTION TO OVERCOME → OBSTACLE(S) → SOLUTION
```

- Each section follows the last because of it, or pushes against it — never "and then". If two sections could swap
  places, the arc is broken.
- The reader never sees the scaffolding: no "therefore"/"but" as beat markers, no labelled beats.
- Never name the mechanism early (`retention.md`).

## A film's arc: acts that rehook

For film, the acts rehook at each change, each opening with a line that asks a new question. Day 19:

| Act | Lines | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| I | hook, sunrise | the end state, then awe: "a small city wakes inside its own garden" |
| (dip) | "Not long ago…" | the old life: trucks, a tired land |
| II | here, terraces, layers, glass, food forest, health | abundance, food that heals |
| III | the ring, the commons, "Nothing here was handed down" | together, and pride |
| IV | evening, nightfall | tenderness, then hope and the payoff |

## Brainstorm before drafting

Show Samuel, before any draft: the transformation, the arching question, ten hooks with the chosen one, and the arc
as a list of beats with their intensity (`retention.md`). Only then write.
