# Gaffer

Light decides what the viewer sees first, what the scene feels like, and whether the picture is believed. The
cinematographer frames; the gaffer makes the light the frame needs — from the place's own sources, made stronger,
softer or warmer, never invented.

## The gaffer's laws

1. **Every source has a reason in the world.** A window, a bulb, the sky: a light the viewer can believe hides every
   cheat (Bradford Young puts practicals in frame so the scene is read from them; Lubezki: natural light registers as
   real even when no one can say why).
2. **Light through the window, from outside** — the direction stays honest and the room stays free of stands (Deakins,
   van Hoytema, Morrison).
3. **Key the far side of the face;** never flat front light. The camera on the room side sees the shadow cheek (the
   short key — Hurlbut, Oseman; the Malick–Lubezki rules: backlight, crosslight near dawn and dusk, never front light).
4. **Subtract before you add.** Walls fill everything; black cloth close to the subject restores the shape (Deakins,
   Hurlbut, Young).
5. **Softness is the source's apparent size; falloff is its distance.** A big source far away is like a small one
   close in — only the falloff differs (Deakins, David Mullen).
6. **Name the ratio in stops:** 1 stop = 2 : 1, 2 stops = 4 : 1, 3 stops = 8 : 1. High-key at 2 : 1 or flatter, low-key
   at 8 : 1 or more; in drama the background often sits 1–2 stops under the key (`setups.md`).
7. **Choose the hour instead of fighting the sun.** The sun's angle shapes faces better than any silk; schedule for it
   (Deakins; Lubezki's *Revenant* in a winter window of daylight and fire).
8. **The practical motivates; a hidden matched source does the work.** Dim the bulb in frame until it reads; light the
   face from its direction with a matched warm source out of shot (Deakins, Lubezki).
9. **Mix colour temperatures on purpose:** warm practicals against cool daylight separate the planes — a natural teal
   and orange made in camera (Hurlbut; Deakins's teal moonlight).
10. **Expose for the face's role, protect the highlights:** a clipped highlight never comes back (Lubezki; Young: dark
    skin glows when underexposed and suffers when overexposed).
11. **Move light only when the world moves it:** drifting sun, a curtain in the breeze, water — never motion for its
    own sake (Deakins on *Blade Runner 2049*).
12. **Write light as an arc and keep relief.** Light carries the story's feeling scene by scene (Storaro); you can't
    spend two hours in a dark palette (Gordon Willis); taking the fill away plays loss of hope, cold to warm plays
    safety (Hurlbut). Ours: struggle in cool north light and a high ratio; turns as a warm practical entering a cool
    space; the vision in low-sun backlight over the dome city.
13. **Keep it simple.** Quality of light first; fewer sources, fewer shadows, faster days (Deakins, his gaffer Billy
    O'Leary).
