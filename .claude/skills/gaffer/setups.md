# Setups, and the numbers behind them

## The setups catalogue

## Stops, distance and size

- One stop doubles the light. Double the distance and a quarter arrives (−2 stops); 1.4× the distance is −1 stop. A soft
  key 0.7 m from the face leaves a wall 2.8 m from the lamp 4 stops darker — that is how a small room drops its
  background.
- The sun is about 0.53° wide: its shadow edge blurs about 0.9 cm per metre between the object and the shadow. An
  overcast sky casts no distinct shadow. A window 1 m from the face is a huge source; at 4 m it looks a quarter as wide
  and gives roughly 1/16 of the light.
- Window against room: the raw difference is 8–10 stops. Push light in, ND the glass, or let the window blow out.

## Faces and exposure

- ARRI's false colour (Rec. 709 view): 18 % grey at 38–42 %, light skin one stop over at 52–56 %, clipping at 99–100 %.
- Ed Lachman's zones: lighter skin +½ to +1 stop over grey, darker skin −½ to −1.
- **Apple Log** (Apple's white paper): 18 % grey → 0.488 (10-bit 500), 90 % white → 0.682 (697), black → 0.150 (154);
  the curve tops out about +6 stops over grey (the small sensor may clip sooner). A grey card at the face reads about
  49 %; put light skin +½ to +1 stop over it; keep the window within about +5 stops if its view should hold.
- On film, Deakins put backlit faces 1–1.5 stops under and front-lit faces about one over, and kept the shadow side
  reading as shadow.

## Colour temperature and gels

- Candle 1,850 K; household incandescent 2,400–2,550 K; tungsten 3,200 K; golden hour about 3,500 K; horizon daylight
  5,000 K; daylight 5,500–6,000 K; overcast 6,500 K; blue sky 15,000 K and up; the sun at the horizon about 2,000 K.
- Gels work in mired (1,000,000 ÷ K): full CTO +159 to +167 (daylight → about 3,000–3,200 K), ½ CTO about +81; full CTB
  −131 to −137 (tungsten → about 5,500–5,700 K). Deakins put ¼–½ blue on tungsten for night interiors.
- Dimming tungsten warms it; dim and warm a tunable LED to copy that. A clear bulb throws cleaner shadows than a frosted
  one.
- Night on screen is lit 100–400× brighter than real moonlight (about 0.3–1 lx) and made to read dark through
  direction, colour, contrast and exposure.

## Samuel's room (14 m², one north window, the Edison bulb, the CB60)

- **The north window is a free book light:** big, soft, cool and steady skylight; direct sun reaches it only early and
  late in high summer.
- **Day:** sit with the window beside you or slightly ahead; the tripod on the room side, looking at the shadow cheek.
  Tape the marks for the chair and the tripod so each day's set-up repeats. The small white room fills itself: hang
  black cloth 0.5–1 m off the shadow side for 4 : 1 or more, or a white sheet on softer days.
- **Lock** ISO, shutter and Kelvin (the Blackmagic Camera app: false colour, zebras, manual white balance, Apple Log).
- **The bulb:** dim it until the glass reads amber around a hot filament; tape its camera side if it flares. Light the
  face with a hidden, matched warm source from the bulb's direction — the CB60 at 2,700–3,200 K, low
  (`neewer-cb60`) — rather than pushing ISO on a small sensor.
- **Night:** fake the window with the CB60 through diffusion or bounced behind the curtain, cooler than the bulb; set
  the white balance between the two (try 4,000–4,500 K) so the window goes cool and the bulb warm (Hurlbut's method; the
  numbers need a test).
- **Handheld:** light the space, not a mark (Libatique).

## Mistakes the pros avoid

- Light with no source in the world; light sandwiches (equal light on both cheeks) and flat front light.
- Filling every shadow until the shadow side no longer reads as shadow.
- Overhead scrims that put the people in different light from the background.
- Practicals that blast white or flare; clipping a highlight you need; overexposing dark skin.
- Haze that drifts between shots; milky blacks; darkness with no relief.
- Green from LEDs or mirrors left for the grade (gel it in camera).
