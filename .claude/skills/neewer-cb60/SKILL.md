# Neewer CB60 RGB

One light, in the room, on its stand by the door: the gaffer's fixture for every scene the window and the Edison bulb
can't carry alone (`gaffer`).

## What it is (official, Neewer)

- **The CB60 RGB** — "CB60 RGB" on its side print and on the label underneath; the back panel's 4th button reads
  HSI/🌡, the 5th FX/CH. (The CB60B looks the same but is bi-colour only; the daylight CB60 has no button row; the
  CB60 PRO has no handle.)
- **70 W**, power supply built in: AC 100–240 V, a detachable 4 m mains cable (IEC inlet on the back), an O/I rocker
  underneath.
- **Light:** 2700–6500 K with G/M −50…+50; HSI (hue 0–360°, saturation 0–100 %); 17 effects; dimming 0–100 %; CRI 97+,
  TLCI 98+; **18,000 lux at 1 m with the standard reflector** (about 8,000 at 1.5 m, 4,500 at 2 m, 2,000 at 3 m).
- **Body:** aluminium, black, 24 × 12.5 × 16 cm, 1.73 kg; a carry handle across the rear top; a Bowens mount with an
  umbrella hole; a single knuckle that tilts it 180° and locks with a lever, on a 5/8" spigot.
- **The stock reflector** (most likely Neewer's RF2): 18 cm across, 14 cm deep, fish-scale facets — a hard ~25° hotspot
  with a dim spill around it. Bare, the head spreads about 110–120° from a 4 cm source.
- **No DMX, no remote in the box, no on-unit presets.** The app keeps favourites and two control-group slots.

## The light's laws

1. **Never leave it as a bare spot in a white room.** Through a softbox, bounced, or as a rim — the reflector's hotspot
   alone is hard sun, not a face light (`recipes.md`).
2. **Motivate it.** It plays the window, the bulb, a TV, a storm — never light from nowhere (`gaffer`, law 1).
3. **Fix the camera's Kelvin, then dial the light** — and keep the shutter at 1/50 (25p) or 1/100 (50p) against 50 Hz
   mains flicker.
4. **Dim in 5 % steps against the zebras or false colour** — skin at about 60–70 % IRE as the key.
5. **Half a metre from anything that burns;** the vents free; unplug before changing a modifier (`recipes.md`,
   "Safety").

## Measure it once (15 minutes with a lux meter)

The bare-head lux, the beam angles, the dimming curve and the colours behind the twin are estimates. On the beam's axis
at 1 m: reflector on, 5600 K at 100, 50, 25 and 10 %; the bare head at 100 %; 2700 K and 6500 K at 100 %; HSI hues 0,
120, 240 at full saturation; the half-brightness circle's diameter on a wall at 2 m; each effect filmed at 50 fps
against a white card. Write the numbers into `twin.md`.
