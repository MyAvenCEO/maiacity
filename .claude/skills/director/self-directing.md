# Directing yourself: a crew of one

Samuel is director, actor, camera operator and editor at once. Everything a crew would do is decided before the take,
because nobody stands behind the camera to catch it during the take. Built on two solo filmmakers' practice
(`inspire-me/move-yourself-not-the-camera/`, `inspire-me/a-vlog-based-on-a-true-story/`) and the `cinematographer`'s
`solo.md` for the camera side.

## Prepare so the day is easy

- **Script → shot list → storyboard.** LifeOfRiza writes the script (or at least its beats), then a shot list as a
  sequence of frames ("top down, close-up, close-up, insert wide, insert face, medium, close-up"), then stick-figure
  storyboards, so she can pop off thirty shots in a day without forgetting a composition. The better the
  pre-production, the better the result — and the faster the edit (`shot-list.md`).
- **Coverage on purpose, not more.** Get what each scene needs — a wide, then the inserts — and a little more, not
  everything. Overshooting makes the edit hard.
- **Know which line goes to which frame.** Say one or two lines to the tripod, then move (handheld, a new place): the
  plan says where each line is said.
- **Choose the song before the shoot** when a scene is built on music; shoot to its beats.

## On the day

- **Control first.** A phone as the monitor: framing, exposure and focus checked before recording (`cinematographer`,
  `solo.md`). Lock what can be locked, so the take is only about the performance.
- **Visualise, set, record, walk back, repeat.** Speed kills quality: slow down, watch every take back, and do it
  again until it is right.
- **Move yourself, not the camera.** Walk into the frame, out of it, towards and away from the lens; the motion links
  shots across the cut. Enter and leave frame on every action you may want to cut on.
- **Do something while you talk** (`storyteller`, `scenes.md`): an activity makes talking to the lens easier and
  moves the scene.
- **Talk to one person.** The lens is a friend in the room, not an audience; the eyeline is the lens itself (or a
  clear look away, never in between).
- **One playable verb per take** — *plead*, *tease*, *confess*, aimed at the one person behind the lens — never a
  result like "be more excited" (Weston). For the next take, change the verb or the action instead of repeating: an
  instinct wears out with repetition (Lumet), and a new action keeps it fresh and honest. Keep the real accidents:
  bloopers and moments that really happen are what make a planned vlog personal.
- **Shoot the emotional beat first,** while it is fresh; set the energy level of every beat before the shoot.
- **Shoot for the edit.** Hold a beat before the first word and after the last; get an insert of the hands and the
  thing; give every action a wide, a medium and a close (`shots.md`).

## Discomfort is part of it

Talking to a camera and filming in public stay uncomfortable for both of them. The answers are speed (the shot list
says what is needed, get it, move on), an activity in the hands, and — when the shot is bigger than one person — one
helper for that shot only (LifeOfRiza's mother pushes the camera; her partner rides the board for a dolly shot).

## Self-directing checklist

- [ ] The script says where each line is said, and what the hands do meanwhile
- [ ] The shot list and the storyboard fit on the day's light (`gaffer`)
- [ ] Framing, exposure and focus checked on the phone before every take
- [ ] Every action covered wide, medium and close; entering and leaving frame
- [ ] Two or three variations of the lines that matter; the real accidents kept
- [ ] Each take watched back before the next set-up
